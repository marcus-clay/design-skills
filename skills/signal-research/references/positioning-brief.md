# Positioning Brief (template)

Used by the Signal Research skill to calibrate content angles. Fill in each section, replace every example with your own material, and keep the file short. Claude should internalize the brief before producing angles and apply it without quoting it mechanically.

All examples below are generic illustrations, marked "Example". Delete them once you have your own answers.

---

## 1. Who I am

Questions:
- What is my role or practice (employee, freelance, consultant, lead, director)?
- How many years of experience, in which kinds of organizations?
- What am I pursuing at the moment (a role, clients, an offer, visibility)?
- In one sentence, what makes my practice distinctive?

Example: "Senior product designer with 10+ years in B2B software, now consulting for mid-size SaaS teams on design systems and AI-assisted workflows."

---

## 2. Credibility map

List the topics where I can write from direct experience, and the evidence behind each. Only these topics get angles that claim authority.

| Topic | Evidence I can share |
|---|---|
| Example: design systems | Built and maintained a token pipeline for a multi-product SaaS |
| Example: AI-assisted prototyping | Weekly use of coding agents in client projects, with before/after timings |
| Example: public sector UX | Two accessibility-driven redesigns of citizen-facing services |
| Example: workshop facilitation | Ran 20+ alignment workshops with mixed product and tech teams |

Topics outside my credibility zone (angles stay observational on these, or are skipped):
- Example: consumer mobile games
- Example: hardware design
- Example: pure visual branding

---

## 3. Audience

Questions:
- Who do I want to reach (roles, seniority, company size, sectors)?
- Who is secondary (recruiters, buyers, peers, students)?
- Who am I explicitly not writing for?
- Which language or languages do I publish in, and on which platform?

Example: "Primary: Heads of Design and CPOs at 50-500 person companies in Europe. Secondary: senior freelancers considering AI adoption. Not for juniors or students. Posts in English on LinkedIn, French for the local network."

---

## 4. Posture and tone

Questions:
- Do I write from doing (builder), from observing (analyst), from teaching, or from challenging (contrarian)?
- How strong are my opinions, and am I comfortable being publicly wrong?
- Do I use numbers, tool names, client contexts (when shareable)?
- Which register fits: peer-to-peer, mentor, expert, storyteller?

Example: "Builder's perspective, analytical but readable, shows reasoning and not only conclusions. Specific over general. Takes positions."

---

## 5. Formats and themes that fit

Formats I want to favor (pick from: Opinion post, Case breakdown, Myth debunking, Observation + question, Tool review, Process documentation, Contrarian take, Data point + implication, Framework reveal, Before/after workflow):
- Example: Process documentation, Before/after workflow, Myth debunking

Recurring themes with high resonance for my audience (3 to 8):
- Example: the gap between a demo and a production workflow
- Example: design maturity as a business variable
- Example: seniority redefined when AI compresses execution
- Example: regulation as a design brief

Formats that fit poorly:
- Example: lists without a position ("5 tools every designer should know")
- Example: pure celebration of a tool

---

## 6. Constraints on tone

Questions:
- Words, phrases or rhetorical moves I dislike (hype vocabulary, clickbait hooks, emojis, hashtags)?
- Confidentiality limits (clients that cannot be named, numbers that cannot be shared)?
- Length or structure preferences?

Example: "No hype, no doom-saying, no emojis. Client names stay anonymous. Posts under 250 words."

---

## 7. Angles to avoid

List the angles the skill should never suggest.

- Example: inspirational posts without substance
- Example: tool-of-the-week takes without a workflow implication
- Example: "AI will replace designers" or its opposite, unless the post adds a real nuance
- Example: advice directed at juniors (if they are not the audience)
- Example: self-promotion without something given to the reader (insight, method, experience)

---

## 8. Positioning statement (optional)

One or two sentences summarizing how I want to be perceived. The skill uses it as a final check on each angle.

Example: "I design products that work in the real world, with AI where it helps and structured process where it does not."
